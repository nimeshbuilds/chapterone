## Start with the reader's age

Open **Kids Books** and choose an age band. The app uses that choice to guide vocabulary, story length, reading level, illustration density, and reader/export typography.

The six bands span ages **1–2, 3–5, 6–8, 9–12, 13–16, and 17–18**. These are writing instructions and display categories, not certified content ratings or a substitute for knowing the individual reader.

## Give the story one clear purpose

For younger children, a single emotional or practical idea is often enough. For example:

> A gentle picture book for a five-year-old about starting at a new school. Mina is nervous about speaking to other children. Let her find one small act of kindness she can manage. Use repetition, a reassuring ending, and no frightening scenes.

For older readers, specify the desired complexity, themes, and boundaries. A short description of the reader's interests can make the story more personal than adding a name alone.

## Add characters

Use **Add character** to enter a name and a brief description: age, role, interests, visual traits, or relationship to the main character. Keep descriptions consistent across the brief and character fields.

You can optionally attach a reference photo to a character. The app resizes it for storage and image requests. **Nano Banana sends selected reference photos to Google** to guide generated illustrations. Use only photos you have permission to share and consider whether a text description is sufficient. Photo references are not a promise of exact likeness across every image.

## Review the plan and pictures

Enable outline review so you can inspect the story beats before drafting. After generation, check the text and every illustration together:

- Does the language fit this reader rather than just the selected age band?
- Are the emotional stakes and ending reassuring or appropriate for the reader?
- Are people, relationships, and backgrounds represented respectfully?
- Do pictures match the events, characters, and details in the text?
- Could any advice, imitation, or scene be unsafe or confusing for a child?

AI output can be unsuitable even when the prompts include safety instructions. An adult should review the whole book before giving it to a child.

## Read or listen together

Use the reader's type and theme controls for comfortable reading. If you configure ElevenLabs, you can choose a separate default voice for children's books in **Settings → Audiobook**. Narration is optional and uses that provider's quota or billing.

Export an [EPUB or PDF](exporting.md) when you want to read on another device. Open the exported file before sharing it to check image placement and type size.
